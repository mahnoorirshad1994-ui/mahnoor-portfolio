Case Study 03

Helping Buyers and Renters Find Agents They Could Trust

CompanyBayut
Year2024
RoleProduct Manager, Consumer Mobile
TypeB2C · Mobile · Two-sided Marketplace
Scroll to explore
The Context

Bayut had already improved listing quality through TruCheck™. But trust in agents remained invisible on both sides of the marketplace.

In the UAE property market, agents compete not just on listings but on responsiveness, visibility, and credibility. With over 20,000 agents on the platform, buyers and renters had no reliable way to know which agent would actually respond, follow through, or be worth contacting. And for agents doing the right thing, there was no way to stand out beyond paid promotions or agency branding. Good behavior went unrecognised. The signal did not exist.

What Users Were Saying
Buyers and Renters

"I never know which agent will actually reply. It is hard to know who is truly active."

Buyers anxious about overpaying needed an agent they could trust to guide them. Renters looking to move quickly needed responsiveness and reliability. Both were making high-stakes decisions with no signal to go on.

Agents

"I respond quickly, update my ads, and follow the rules. It would be great if buyers could actually see that."

Agents who were doing the right thing had no way to differentiate themselves from those who were not. The only lever was paid visibility, which rewarded spend over performance.

Both sides of the marketplace had the same problem. Trust was invisible. And invisible trust cannot influence decisions.

The Problem and the Hypothesis

There was no way to recognise top-performing agents, motivate good behaviour at scale, or give buyers and renters trust signals beyond listing badges. The original proposal was to create a new badge, similar to TruCheck™. I proposed a different direction.

Turn trust into a motivating system. If buyers and renters could see verified signals of agent quality at the exact moment they were choosing who to contact, they would engage more with high-performing agents. Agents would notice. Behaviour would change. The platform would become more trusted for everyone.

The Approach — A Two-Sided Market Strategy
Supply side — Agents
Demand side — Buyers and Renters
01
Visibility as the incentive
TruBroker™ gave agents something no paid product could: earned visibility. Agents who met the criteria for the TruBroker™ badge were ranked higher in the Find My Agent section, surfaced more prominently on listing cards, and distinguished from the rest of the market by a signal that buyers and renters had come to associate with genuine quality.
The signal
TruPoints earned for responsiveness, listing accuracy, and deal follow-through. Badge status tied directly to TruPoints balance and ongoing behaviour.
02
Trust at the moment of decision
I owned the consumer app experience for TruBroker™. The badge appeared on listing cards, where buyers and renters were making contact decisions. The Find My Agent section gave them a dedicated way to search agents directly. Agent profiles surfaced badge credentials, response rates, and area expertise clearly on mobile. The goal was to make the right choice feel obvious, not effortful.
What this meant for users
You will not get ignored. You will not get misled. This agent has earned the badge and stands to lose it if they do not deliver.
03
Rewarding agents who delivered better service
43% of all TruPoints were attributed to WhatsApp responsiveness alone. The system measured what buyers and renters actually cared about and rewarded agents for it. Bayut also provided badge holders with additional resources: training materials, dedicated account support, and early access to platform features. Holding the badge was worth maintaining.
04
Educating without interrupting
Buyers and renters who had not seen TruBroker™ before needed to understand what it meant without being slowed down. A lightweight tooltip on first encounter and a dedicated explanation within the agent profile answered the question for those who wanted it, and stayed out of the way for those who did not.
The Flywheel
Buyers and renters engage with TruBroker™ agents → Lead volume increases for badge holders → Agents compete to earn and maintain the badge → Service quality improves across the platform → Trust grows on both sides
What Made It Hard
Making trust visible without overloading the UI
The listing card is already dense with information. Adding a badge and agent context required careful prioritisation of what to show and what to leave out. Too much information at this stage would slow the browsing experience. Too little would make the badge meaningless. The placement was a deliberate choice, not a default.
Coordinating across platforms
A buyer who saw a TruBroker™ badge on the app and then visited on web needed to see the same information and the same trust signals. Inconsistency would have undermined the credibility of the badge itself. This required close coordination with the web product team throughout the build, not just at handoff.
Localising the experience
Abu Dhabi and Dubai had different badge requirements due to market differences in listing behaviour and responsiveness patterns. The consumer app had to handle these variations without confusing buyers or renters, which meant conditional logic and variable UI states that had to be thoroughly tested across both markets.
The leaderboard on mobile
On mobile, space is limited. The Find My Agent leaderboard had to lead with the most trust-relevant signals — badge status, response rate, area expertise — while making it easy to access the full agent profile for users who wanted more detail before committing to a contact action. Every additional data point competed for attention.

What I Took Away

This project reinforced something I had seen before: in a two-sided marketplace, the consumer experience and the supply-side incentive system are not separate problems. The design of the badge on the listing card directly shaped how much agents cared about earning it. When buyers and renters responded to the signal, agents noticed, and behaviour changed. Building the consumer experience well was not just a UX decision. It was what made the entire system work.
